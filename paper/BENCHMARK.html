Benchmarking Without Labels

What the published flood-susceptibility literature actually measures, why its scores are not comparable to ours, and the protocol for carrying a model into Al Madinah — a city with no flood records at all.

Mityaf · مِطياف 17 papers read 14 models under test 2026-08-29

READ THIS FIRSTTheir 0.98 and our 0.40 are not the same measurement

Nearly every flood-susceptibility paper reports ROC-AUC above 0.90. Ours is 0.61. That gap is almost entirely an artefact of how the datasets are built — not of model quality — and understanding why is the single most useful thing in this document.

The field's usual setup
AUC 0.95–0.99
  • Balanced sampling. Vemula 2025 draws 1,247 flooded points and exactly 1,247 non-flooded ones — a 1:1 ratio invented by the analyst.
  • Random splits. Seleem 2022 splits 60/20/20 at random, so a test cell can sit 2 m from a training cell.
  • Non-flood points chosen to be easy. Often sampled far from any known flood, which makes the two classes trivially separable.
  • One seed, no intervals. Almost never repeated.
Mityaf's setup
PR-AUC 0.40 · lift 1.44×
  • Natural prevalence. 27.4% of NYC road cells flood, 35.8% in Chicago. Nothing rebalanced.
  • Spatial blocking. GroupKFold on 0.05° (~5.5 km) blocks, so no test cell borders a training cell.
  • Hard negatives. Every negative is a cell with >50 m of road — the model cannot win by answering "is there a road here?"
  • Cross-city transfer as the primary endpoint, five seeds.
The number that proves the point

Agostini, Pierson & Garg (AAAI 2024) model exactly our data — NYC 311 storm-flooding complaints — and report AUC 0.642 for their best model, against 0.520 for the homogeneous-reporting baseline. Our cross-city ROC-AUC is 0.609–0.652.

When someone models the real, unbalanced, under-reported problem, they land exactly where we land. The 0.98s come from a different problem.

02Why a cross-study leaderboard would be dishonest

You asked for a leaderboard. Here is the uncomfortable answer: ranking these studies against each other would be meaningless, and four pieces of published evidence say so.

Evidence 1 — equal AUC, completely different maps

Vakhshoori & Zare (2018) built susceptibility maps that scored exactly the same AUC of 0.77 yet disagreed on where the risk was. Maps from the same method with only a different random seed differed in at least 29% of pixels. No pair of maps with equal ROC-AUC reached a Cohen's kappa above 0.61 against each other.

Evidence 2 — AUC 0.80 with kappa 0.02

Seleem 2022 tested Berlin models on an area outside the training region. The ANN at 30 m held AUC 0.80 — respectable-looking — while its kappa fell to 0.02, meaning agreement no better than chance. AUC survived; the map was worthless.

Why this matters for you specifically

Al Madinah is an out-of-area test. Seleem's is the closest published analogue to what you are doing, and the honest reading of it is that out-of-area performance degrades substantially and that AUC will hide it. This is the argument for reporting lift and precision@K, which do not hide it.

Evidence 3 — the sampling choice outweighs the model choice

Zhang 2025 changed only how non-flood points were selected — frequency-ratio plus one-class SVM instead of random buffer sampling — and accuracy moved 24 percentage points. No model swap in this entire literature moves anything by more than about 3. Your negative-sampling rule deserves its own experiment more than your classifier does.

Evidence 4 — no model family reliably wins

Random Forest wins in Berlin, Seoul, New Orleans and Cincinnati. XGBoost wins in Yancheng and on the French Riviera. CatBoost wins in Romania. In Beijing, Han 2024 reports RF and SVM-RBF actively overfitting and a PSO-WELLSVM variant winning instead. Typical separation between the top two or three models is 0.01–0.03 AUC — inside the noise of a single split.

1. Negative-sampling experiment — the literature says it moves results 8× more than the model does. 2. PU learning — it is the correct framing and the reviewer objection you cannot otherwise answer. 3. OOD detection — it is the only way to say something defensible about Madinah.

The awkward early result

Logistic regression is beating XGBoost on transfer — 1.329 against 1.277 — while losing within-city, 1.336 against 1.437. That is boosting memorising city-specific structure and a linear model generalising past it.

If it survives five seeds and four cities, it matters: transfer is the only endpoint that justifies deploying in Madinah, and the incumbent may be the wrong choice for the claim the product actually makes. Nine features is a small space, and linear models are hard to beat in small spaces.

05The Madinah protocol — modelling a city with no labels

This is the part that matters. Al Madinah has zero recorded flood incidents, so no model can be trained or tested on it. Here is the defensible way to proceed, in order.

  1. Never train on Madinah. Say so loudly.

    train_multi.py already excludes any city without a labels_url. Madinah supplies features and consumes a model — 8,647 cells, 30,337 segments, zero labels. Keep that exclusion explicit in code, not just in prose.

  2. Make leave-one-city-out the headline, not within-city CV.

    Within-city spatial blocking answers "does this generalise across neighbourhoods". It cannot answer "does this generalise across cities", because drainage design, climate and reporting culture are held constant — and those are exactly what differ in Madinah. Only LOCO tests the claim you actually make.

  3. Get past n = 2 cities.

    Two cities give two transfer numbers, and a reviewer will call that anecdotal. San Francisco (31,821 flooding complaints with clean lat/long) and Dallas (~3,500 street-flood complaints with coordinates) are verified available, and their Copernicus DEM tiles exist. Four to five cities makes the transfer claim an actual distribution.

  4. Report the transfer degradation, do not hide it.

    Within-city 1.44× falls to 1.24× across cities. State that number as the expected Madinah performance. Seleem 2022 is the precedent: out-of-area AUC fell to 0.80 with kappa 0.02, and reporting that honestly is what makes the rest credible.

  5. Flag out-of-distribution segments.

    Compute Mahalanobis distance from each Madinah cell to the pooled training distribution, plus a per-feature Population Stability Index, and ship an ood_flag beside risk_pct. The platform can then say "this street is unlike anything the model has seen" rather than silently extrapolating. This is the single most direct answer to the obvious reviewer objection.

  6. Output a rank, never a probability.

    You already do this — risk_pct is a percentile. Keep it. A model transferred across domains is not calibrated, and Madinah has no labels to calibrate against. A rank is defensible; a probability would be a lie with a decimal point.

  7. Validate against something, even if not labels.

    No flood inventory does not mean no ground truth at all. Municipal tanker dispatch records, resident survey responses, and post-storm imagery each give a partial check on whether the top-ranked segments are the ones that actually pond. Any of these turns "untested" into "partially corroborated".

06What to report, so nobody can dismiss it

  • PR-AUC and lift over prevalence, not ROC-AUC and accuracy. You are already ahead of the field here — exactly one paper found (Ajin 2025) reports average precision, and none report lift.
  • Accuracy alongside the always-negative baseline. 60.7% next to 72.6% looks like a loss until you show the 72.6% model catches zero floods.
  • Precision@K, because that is how the platform is used — a ranked list worked down until budget runs out, not a threshold applied to 30,337 segments.
  • Five seeds, mean ± sd, on every number. Your feature importances move ±1.7 points between seeds; a single-seed table invites exactly the challenge you cannot answer.
  • The prevalence of every dataset, so a reader can convert your numbers to theirs and back.
  • Pinned library versions. XGBoost 3.4.1 gives PR-AUC 0.342 where the older version gave 0.340 — small, but it means an unpinned environment cannot reproduce a published table.
The one-sentence positioning

"We report a 1.24× cross-city lift under spatial blocking at natural prevalence, where the field reports 0.95+ AUC on artificially balanced samples with random splits — and we do so because ours is the only setup that resembles deploying in a city with no flood records."
