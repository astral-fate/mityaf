From Satellite to Street

Exactly what مِطياف does, end to end — every abbreviation spelled out, every stage shown in a picture drawn from the real data, and an honest account of where machine learning does and does not sit.

30,337 streets · 2 data sources · 9 numbers each · 0 Madinah labels
The one-paragraph answer

We cut Madinah's road network into 30,337 pieces. For each piece we compute nine numbers from two free sources: a satellite elevation model and the public world map. Those nine numbers put every street in order, from most to least likely to hold standing water. Then a set of rules — not a learned model — labels 2,617 of them with why: a hollow, a runoff path, or an underpass. Machine learning was used to prove the nine numbers carry signal, not to produce the ranking that ships.

THE SHAPE OF ITThe whole system on one page

STAGE 1What a "street segment" is

A road is not one thing. طريق الملك عبدالله runs for kilometres, and it does not flood uniformly along its length.

So we cut the network into pieces. Each piece is roughly 174 metres of road, and we call it a segment. Madinah's 5,280 km network becomes 30,337 segments. Every segment gets its own row, its own nine numbers, and its own score.

That is the unit of everything downstream. When the site says "2,617 streets need inspection", it means 2,617 of these pieces — not 2,617 named roads.

STAGE 2The two sources — see them for yourself

DEMDigital Elevation Model

A free dataset from the European Space Agency giving the height of the ground across the whole world, on a grid of 30-metre squares. It is not a photograph. It is a table of numbers — one height per square.

Water runs downhill and pools in low spots, so height is the physical basis for everything. Six of our nine numbers are computed from it. Here is Madinah's, lit from the north-west so the landforms read:

Zoom into one 450-metre window around the resident's pin and the "grid of numbers" claim becomes literal — this is the raw data, printed:

OSMOpenStreetMap

A free map of the world, made by volunteers — like Wikipedia, but for geography. Anyone can add a road, name it, or tag it. We use it for four things:

  • The road lines themselves — this is what we cut into 30,337 segments
  • Road type — motorway, primary, residential. Gives major_frac
  • How much road is packed into an area — gives road_density
  • Tunnel tags — tunnel=yes, which produced the 47
OSM is not ground truth for flooding

This is the confusion worth clearing up. OSM tells us where roads are and what kind they are. It contains no information whatsoever about whether a street floods. It is an input, never a label and never an answer.

We are also not "under-using" it — we use everything in it that bears on this problem. Its limits are real though: it does not contain Madinah's storm-drain network, and it is missing several informal district names (حي الغربية, الجبور, المعيقلة, مخطط باقدو), which is why those districts cannot be checked at all.

STAGE 4Ranking — and yes, all 30,337

Every single segment gets a score. None are skipped.

The score is a percentile: 0.99 means "more prone to holding water than 99% of Madinah's streets". It is not a probability — it does not mean a 99% chance of flooding. It is a position in a queue, not a forecast.

We produce four such rankings from the same nine numbers, and they disagree with each other. The one that ships is the hydrology ranking, built purely from the physics — because it is the one that found the basin the resident's three coordinates landed in.

STAGE 5Classification — a different job, done by rules

Ranking says how bad. Classification says why — and therefore what to do about it. These are separate outputs covering different numbers of streets.

Depression — 533 segments

A closed hollow below the local drainage line. Water arrives and has no way out. This is the King Fahd basin.

rule: sink > 0.05 m AND hand < 0

Convergence — 2,084 segments

Lots of water arriving onto ground too flat to shed it, but above the drainage line. Floods during the storm, drains after. This is شارع البخاري.

rule: logacc ≥ 90th percentile AND slope < 1°

Tunnel — 47 ways

The lowest point on its road by construction, not by terrain. Read from a map tag, because the satellite cannot see it.

rule: OSM tunnel=yes OR layer < 0
Ranking and classification are not the same count

30,337 segments are ranked. Only 2,617 are classified — the other 27,720 match none of the three rules.

And none of this is machine learning. All three rules are simple if-this-then-that tests on the nine numbers, written by hand from physics. You can check any of them by eye.

STAGE 6What the 47 tunnels are for

They are not training data, not labels, and not among the 30,337 segments. They are a third list of at-risk places, found a different way.

We only looked because a Saudi government source made us. In May 2015 the Saudi Press Agency reported an engineering project at نفق الملك عبدالله to drain «تجمع مائي حاد داخل النفق» — severe water accumulation inside the tunnel — specifically so vehicles would stop being submerged. The tunnel=yes tag had been sitting in OSM the whole time and our pipeline was ignoring it.

When we added the rule, that same tunnel came out 2nd of 47. The method independently ranked near the top the one underpass a government source had already flagged as dangerous.

It matters because this is the mechanism with the worst consequences. Standing water in a residential street costs cars and time; a flooded motorway underpass drowns people.

STAGE 7Where machine learning actually sits

This is the part that changed during the work, and the honest answer is not the one we started with.

Madinah has zero recorded flood incidents. Nothing to train on. So we trained on four American cities that do keep records — New York, Chicago, San Francisco, Dallas — 130,998 citizen flood complaints between them, using the identical nine numbers.

Then we tested it the only honest way: LOCO — train on three cities, test on the fourth it has never seen. That simulates pointing it at Madinah.

What we askedWhat we found
Do the nine numbers carry real signal?Yes — and this is what licenses using them on a city with no records
Which of 14 models is best?CatBoost, but the whole spread is small and the ranking is unstable
Does the trained model beat plain physics in Madinah?No. At the verified basin: hydrology 0.99, trained model 0.63
Is the American pipeline worth its complexity?It buys roughly 5% over sorting by road density alone

So the machine learning got demoted, deliberately. It stays for two real jobs: it proved the feature set transfers between cities, and it produces the per-segment explanations behind the Arabic rationale_ar. The ranking that ships is physics.

Why the model loses in Madinah

We never trained on "where water stands". We trained on where people complained. In dense American cities those correlate well enough to score. But complaints follow people, traffic and reporting habits — which is why road_density dominates the model, and why it under-ranks a quiet residential hollow that floods for three days.

STAGE 8What the system does not know

QuestionAnswer
Will this street flood on Tuesday?No. There is no forecast, and no link to a specific storm.
How deep will the water be?No. Depth is never predicted.
What is the probability of flooding?No. Every number is a rank, not a probability.
Has it been tested in Madinah?No. Zero labels exist there. Every ranking is a prediction.
Where is the storm-drain network?Unknown. Not in OSM. This is the highest-value missing input.
Does it cover حي الغربية?No — that district is missing from OSM, and it holds the only documented five-day flood.

What we do have from Madinah

45 dated flood reports, of which 19 are independently corroborated against two rainfall records, and 3 precise coordinates from a resident. Those are not training data — they are checks, gathered afterwards to test whether the ranking points at places that really flood.

The sentence that survives cross-examination

"We rank Madinah's 30,337 street segments by physical susceptibility to standing water, classify 2,617 of them by mechanism so the remedy is clear, and flag 47 underpasses the elevation model cannot see. Machine learning is how we proved the method transfers between cities — the ranking that ships is hydrology. Nothing has been validated in Madinah, because Madinah has no flood records; the 19 corroborated reports we gathered are the first."
